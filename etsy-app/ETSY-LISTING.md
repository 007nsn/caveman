# Etsy Listing Kit — Budget Planner & Expense Tracker

Everything you need to list the Budget Planner on Etsy as an **instant
digital download**. Copy it, adjust the wording to suit your shop, and paste.

## What the buyer gets

A ZIP file, `BudgetPlanner.zip`, containing:

- `BudgetPlanner.html` — the planner itself. It runs in any computer browser
  (Chrome, Edge, Safari, Firefox) on Windows, Mac or Chromebook, and works
  fully offline.
- `How-To-Open.pdf` — a one-page getting-started guide (source:
  `HOW-TO-OPEN.html`).

**Computer only, for now.** Phones and tablets open downloaded HTML files in
a preview mode that may not run the planner. Don't advertise phone or tablet
support unless the planner is hosted at a private link the buyer can open
in mobile Safari or Chrome.

**Keep the ZIP out of public places.** Don't commit it to a public repo or
post it anywhere. Upload it only to the Etsy listing.

## Getting paid and delivering the file

You don't need Stripe, Zelle or your own PayPal account to sell on Etsy.
**Etsy Payments** handles checkout and deposits your earnings into your bank
account.

**One-time shop setup**

1. Create an Etsy account and open a shop (Sell on Etsy → Get started).
   Etsy may charge a one-time setup fee; the amount is shown during setup.
2. Under **Payments**, choose your country and link your bank account for
   payouts. A Citibank checking account works.
3. Verify your identity when Etsy asks (government ID). Use your legal name.
4. Add a debit or credit card for billing. Etsy charges listing fees to it
   whenever your sales balance can't cover them.
5. Create the listing (see below), upload `BudgetPlanner.zip` under
   **Digital files**, then publish.

**What happens on each sale (automatic)**

1. The buyer pays by card, Apple Pay, Google Pay or PayPal. Etsy offers
   all of these at checkout; you don't set them up.
2. Etsy confirms the payment.
3. **Only then** does Etsy unlock the download. The buyer gets an email and
   a Download Files button on their Purchases page. Before payment, the
   file is not visible or downloadable to anyone.
4. You get a sale notification. Your auto-sent buyer message (below) goes
   out.
5. Etsy deposits the money, minus fees, to your bank on your payout
   schedule. New shops may have part of their funds held for a while
   before payout.

**Why not Zelle:** Zelle is for paying people you know. Citi's Zelle terms
prohibit business use on personal accounts, it offers no purchase
protection, and you'd have to email every file by hand after checking each
payment.

**Selling outside Etsy as well (optional):** Payhip pays you through PayPal
or Stripe, which you connect. Lemon Squeezy pays out to your bank directly
and handles sales tax for you. Both deliver the file automatically after
payment, the same way Etsy does.

## Listing images (Etsy allows 10)

Buyers decide from the photos. Use **real screenshots of the app**, since
Etsy expects listing images to show the actual product. Suggested set:

1. Hero shot: the app on a laptop, light mode, with realistic sample data
2. The same screen in dark mode
3. Close-up: summary cards (income / expenses / balance / savings rate)
4. Close-up: category donut chart + budget progress bars
5. What's in the ZIP: the planner plus the PDF guide
6. "How it works" graphic: download → unzip → open → track
7. Feature checklist graphic
8. "100% private: your data stays on your computer" trust graphic

To capture them, open `BudgetPlanner.html`, add sample data, and use your
computer's screenshot tool. Placing a real screenshot inside a laptop frame
(Canva has free ones) is fine.
